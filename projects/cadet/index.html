CADeT

Causal-Aware Deformation Transmission for Indirect Robotic Manipulation of Soft Tissue

arXiv preprint · September 2026

Junlei Hu, Dominic Jones, Pietro Valdastri

STORM Lab · University of Leeds

Read the paper ↗PDF ↗Watch experiments ↓BibTeX

Manipulate an accessible tissue to move a target that the robot cannot grasp directly. CADeT uses tissue responses and targeted probing to infer how deformation is transmitted, then adapts indirect shape control to that interaction.

The same target response can have different causes

A target that barely moves may be disconnected from the manipulated tissue, or attached but blocked by its surroundings. CADeT maintains a belief over three transmission modes:

Mode Interaction Target response
Decoupled Ineffective proxy–target coupling Little transmitted motion
Coupled The proxy transmits deformation The target responds to proxy motion
Blocked The coupled target meets an external constraint Motion is restricted
  1. Observe and infer. Track the proxy and target tissues, updating the transmission-mode belief from their responses.
  2. Probe when ambiguous. Choose an additional action whose predicted responses help distinguish competing modes.
  3. Control through the proxy. Combine the belief with an online estimate of the adhesion Jacobian in model predictive control.

Method details: Sections IV–VI of the paper.

Results at a glance

In the paper's simulation evaluation, full CADeT achieved:

99.3%mode-identification accuracy
98.0%manipulation success
30.1 smean task time (± 2.7 s)

Simulation results: 149/150 trials correctly identified. Manipulation statistics are reported separately in Table III. Source: Section VII, Tables I and III.

Physical experiments used the da Vinci Research Kit with tissue phantoms and ex vivo porcine tissues. In the three nominal reachable Coupled cases, final mean shape errors were 2.1–2.7 mm (Table V).

Scope. Correctly identifying coupling does not make every target shape reachable. The experiments include disconnected, constrained and reachability-limited cases. The method assumes a predefined grasp and does not provide a formal tissue-safety guarantee.
